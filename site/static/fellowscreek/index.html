Fellows Creek — Project Index

A browser-based, collaborative watershed game developed with the San Mateo Resource Conservation District (SMRCD). This page summarises the game and links to every design document produced so far.

Overview
The setting
A small coastal California creek system modelled on Pescadero Creek in San Mateo County — headwaters, a farm valley, and a floodplain town at the estuary, laid out as a square-tile board where every tile knows what lies downstream.
The format
A facilitated stakeholder workshop of 60–90 minutes, and online synchronous. Four players take turns; major actions happen on your turn, minor actions any time. Wet and dry seasons shift the pressures on the creek. Playable in English and Spanish.
The core decision
When a log falls in the creek, the player whose reach it lands in chooses to leave it, cut it and send it downstream, or remove it entirely. Each choice cascades into shared water, habitat, and infrastructure systems — and onto the other players.
Four asymmetric roles, each with one skill no one else has.
Farmer
Needs reliable irrigation through wet and dry seasons without drawing the creek below fish-passage levels.
SkillBuild a storage pond to bank wet-season water.
Ecologist
Wants large blocking logs left in place, healthy riparian buffers, and enough flow for salmon.
SkillAnchor logs to the bank.
Road Manager
Keeps culverts and bridges intact — and pays when drifting debris from upstream jams them.
SkillInstall a debris deflector at a crossing.
Land Manager
Protects managed land and the town from storms made worse by an elevated flood-risk track.
SkillOpen a floodplain to slow flood pulses.
Core design tension
The cheap short-term fix — cutting or removing wood, drawing more water — helps the acting player and harms others now or later. The best collective outcome depends on trust, coordination, and reading the creek correctly.
